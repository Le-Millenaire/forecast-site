How it works

A forecasting model is not one more poll. It gathers what can be measured: published polls, past results, incumbency, fundraising. From these it derives a distribution of possible outcomes, with their probability.

Three engines

House, Senate and governors each have their own engine, validated on past midterms. All start from the same national environment, the likely-voter generic ballot, recomputed daily.

Past polling errors

Polls missed in every midterm, most often in favour of Democrats. The model replays the size of these errors in both directions. It does not assume in advance that one side will be underestimated.

Two hundred thousand simulations

Each chamber is replayed 200,000 times, with a national error shared by every race and an error specific to each race. The probability shown is the share of simulations in which the event happens.

What remains outside the calculation

The link between House and Senate cannot be measured over four midterms: the page gives an exact range rather than a figure. Candidates outside the top two are bounded by the polls, and Alaska and Maine (governors) are not yet probabilized.

What the model measuresThree independent engines, validated out of sample on past midterms. House: each district's 2024 result, incumbency and the national generic ballot, with a national error calibrated on polling history. Senate: race polls (56%), state history and candidate profile (44%). Governors: structural formula (2024 presidential vote, national climate and an incumbent governor on the ballot); polls enter from 13 October, a horizon validated on four elections.
What it does not say yetChances are computed between Democrat and Republican, or between independent and Republican where the independent is the main challenger (Idaho, Montana, Nebraska, South Dakota). 14 districts (Louisiana, same-party runoffs) remain outside the full field. In the Senate, a third candidate's chance is bounded by the polls: below 0.5% in Alaska, Maine and Nebraska; at most: Montana 1.7% (Alani Bankhead, D). The joint House and Senate probability is given as an exact range: the dependence between the two contests cannot be measured from available history.
